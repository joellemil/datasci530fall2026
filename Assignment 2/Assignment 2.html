Executive Summary¶

This analysis examines Formula 1 constructor performance between 1981 and 2020, focusing on championship points, rankings and Ferrari’s performance over the period. The three constructors with the highest accumulated points across the 40-year period were Ferrari, Mercedes and McLaren, providing an overview of the teams that achieved the strongest overall results. Comparing the rankings for 1981–2020 with 2001–2020 shows how the leading constructors changed over time, with Red Bull entering the top three and replacing McLaren.

The analysis also compares the top three constructors with the average accumulated points across all constructors during the period. This shows how substantially the leading constructors' performances differed from the average constructor total. Ferrari was then examined separately to identify its highest-scoring season, which was 2018, its number of different drivers between 1981 and 2020, which was 25, and changes in its championship position across the period.

Overall, the findings provide a long-term view of constructor performance in Formula 1 and demonstrate how performance varied both between constructors and across different periods. The Ferrari analysis provides a more detailed example of how the performance of an individual constructor changed over time.

a) i. Which 3 constructors earned the most points between 1981 and 2020, and how many total points did each of them earn?¶

constructor_standings contains the points earned by each constructor at each race, but does not contain the year of the race. I merged it with races using raceId to add the year and round variables. This allowed me to filter the data to the required 1981–2020 period and order races within each season.

I sorted the data by constructor, year, and race round so that the final race for each constructor in each season could be identified. I then kept the final observation for each constructor-year combination because points is cumulative throughout the season, meaning the final observation represents the constructor's total points for that season. Finally, I summed these final season totals across 1981–2020 and ranked the constructors by their overall points.

I selected the ranking, constructor name, and total points to show the three constructors with the highest total points between 1981 and 2020. The points have been formatted with commas and I removed the DataFrame index to make the table easier to read.

# Merge races with constructor_standings to add year and round
constructor_data = constructor_standings.merge(
    races[['raceId', 'year', 'round']],
    on='raceId'
)

# Keep races from 1981 to 2020 and sort by season
constructor_data = constructor_data[
    constructor_data['year'].between(1981, 2020)
].sort_values(['constructorId', 'year', 'round'])

# Get final points for each constructor in each season
final_points = (
    constructor_data
    .groupby(['constructorId', 'year'])
    .tail(1)
)

# Calculate total points across all seasons
total_points = (
    final_points
    .groupby('constructorId')['points']
    .sum()
    .reset_index(name='Total Points')
)

# Add constructor names and select the top 3
top_3 = (
    total_points
    .merge(constructors[['constructorId', 'name']], on='constructorId')
    .sort_values('Total Points', ascending=False)
    .head(3)
    .reset_index(drop=True)
)

# Add ranking and rename columns
top_3.insert(0, 'Ranking', range(1, 4))
top_3 = top_3.rename(columns={'name': 'Constructor'})

# Display total points and ranking for the top 3
top_3[['Ranking', 'Constructor', 'Total Points']].style.hide(axis='index') \
    .format({'Total Points': '{:,.0f}'}) \
    .set_caption('Top 3 Constructors by total points, 1981 - 2020')

I retained constructors that appeared in only one period and labelled their ranking as “Not in Top 3” in the other period, rather than excluding them from the comparison. This makes it possible to clearly identify constructors that entered or left the top three between the two time periods.

I chose to compare the rankings based on total points in each period, as this provides a clear indication of how consistently constructors performed at the top of the standings across the two periods.
